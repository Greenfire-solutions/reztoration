# Reztoration protocols

These rules come before "as much information as possible". Every agent and every import follows them.

1. **The nation speaks first.** A nation's own website, culture department, museum, language program and historic preservation office go at the top of its page, ahead of any outside archive.
2. **Sacred stays out.** Anything marked sacred, ceremonial, restricted, seasonal, gender-restricted or community-only is not loaded, even where an archive has made it public. The collection is noted as restricted so the page can point people to the community's own process.
3. **Labels are kept exactly.** Rights statements and Local Contexts Traditional Knowledge (TK) and Biocultural (BC) labels are recorded as the source gives them. A license is never invented; unknown is written as unknown.
4. **Links, not copies.** Photos, audio and documents stay with the institution that holds them. The map stores the link, the thumbnail link and the rights text.
5. **Public and polite only.** No logins, no paywalls, no getting around robots.txt or access requests, about one request a second per site.
6. **Plant medicine is a historical record.** Shown with its citation, never as advice, never with doses added.
7. **Correction and removal on request.** If someone from a nation says a thing is wrong or should not be shown, it comes out first and gets discussed after.
8. **Outlines are approximate.** Territory outlines (Native Land Digital) overlap and are a starting point, not a legal boundary. Their terms of use apply; see data/research/nativeland-terms.md.

Grounding: the CARE Principles for Indigenous Data Governance (Collective benefit, Authority to control, Responsibility, Ethics) and Local Contexts (localcontexts.org).

## Treaties

9. **"Broken" needs a finding, not an opinion.** A treaty is shown as broken only when at least one breach is documented by a court ruling, the Indian Claims Commission or Court of Claims, an Act of Congress or executive action that took or overrode what the treaty secured, or the US government's own admission, each with a source the researcher actually opened. A nation's own statement is shown, attributed to the nation, and marks the treaty "contested" until a finding is found. Signed treaties the Senate never ratified are shown as "never ratified". Nothing is ever labelled "honored"; a treaty with no documented breach is "no breach found yet".
10. **The nation's account sits beside the government's.** Where a nation has published its own history of a treaty, it is linked first on the treaty card.
11. **Cession areas are Royce's 1899 survey** as digitised by the US Forest Service (public domain). They are the government's record of what was taken, not the nation's.

## Native businesses

12. **Native ownership must be stated by someone credible.** A business is listed only when its Native or tribal ownership is stated by the Indian Arts and Crafts Board Source Directory, a tribe's own list of its enterprises, a certifying or member body (Intertribal Agriculture Council, AIHEC, a Native CDFI network, NCAIED and similar), or the business itself naming the nation it belongs to. The basis is recorded on every listing.
13. **Business places only.** Storefronts, offices and farms open to the public get an address; a person working from home is placed at their town, never their street. No personal phone numbers or emails beyond what the business publishes for customers.
14. **Closed businesses come off.** Every listing carries the date it was last checked.
